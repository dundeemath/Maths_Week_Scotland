Suppose you wanted to identify the shortest possible route from start to finish, i.e. how much further did you just walk than you needed to? Real versions of this problem sit behind delivery route planning, circuit board drilling, and DNA sequencing.

This is the Travelling Salesman Problem (TSP) – one of the most famous problems in the whole of mathematics and computer science. It sounds almost too simple to be interesting: just try every possible order of stops and keep the shortest one. The catch is how many orders there are. With 5 stops there are only 12 distinct routes to check. With 10 stops there are already 181,440. With 20 stops there are over 60 quadrillion – so many that a computer checking a billion routes every single second would still need about two years to get through them all. Trying every option, which works fine for a handful of stops, becomes hopeless almost immediately – and no computer, however fast, escapes that by brute force alone.

The app below lets you compare four routes side by side, using the toggle in the sidebar:

  • The route you walked today – shown by default, in the order you actually visited the stops.
  • Your simulated route – click stops to select them (at least 3), then switch to “Adds the next stop to your route” and click your selected stops again in whatever order you think gives the shortest trip.
  • Algorithm route – a fast algorithm’s guess (nearest neighbour, optionally cleaned up with a technique called 2-opt).
  • Optimal route – since there are only 5 stops here, the exact best route, found by checking every possibility.

Switch between them and see how close your own guess – or your own feet – got to optimal.
